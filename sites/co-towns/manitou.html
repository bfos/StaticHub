Overview

Manitou Springs is a singular place in Colorado — a small city of 5,500 that has maintained a fully independent commercial district for over 150 years, zero chain businesses, and a distinctive arts-hippie-bohemian identity that has survived being surrounded by Colorado Springs on three sides and a constant stream of tourists interested in Pikes Peak on the fourth. The mineral springs that gave the town its name still flow from several dozen public springs throughout the commercial district; residents still drink from them. It is genuinely weird and genuinely wonderful.

The walkability is real and unusual for its size — the commercial corridor along Manitou Avenue and the surrounding streets can be covered on foot, and the town is compact enough that car-free daily life is achievable in a way it isn't anywhere else in the Colorado Springs metro. The Manitou Incline (a 2,768-step former funicular track up the side of the mountain) draws fitness-obsessed residents and tourists daily, but it's a community amenity as much as a tourist attraction — locals use it regularly.

The limitation is size and isolation from other city amenities. Manitou is surrounded by Colorado Springs, which provides services but also shapes the cultural context — you're in the most conservative large city in Colorado, 5 minutes from one of its quirkiest enclaves. For everyday independence, this mostly works. For cultural life beyond what the 5,500-person Manitou commercial district can provide, you're relying on the Colorado Springs ecosystem (see that page).

Skiing

No ski resort near Manitou Springs. Breckenridge is about 2 hours northwest; Monarch is about 2.5 hours. For dedicated skiers, Manitou is not the right base. The Pikes Peak area has snowshoe and backcountry opportunities in winter, and the Incline is an excellent winter fitness option, but resort skiing requires genuine travel commitment.

Culture & Feel

Every business in Manitou's commercial district is independently owned — this is enforced by local ordinance and community pressure and has held for generations. Adam's Mountain Café is the beloved local breakfast institution, a James Beard-level serious vegetarian/organic menu in a hippie-chic room. The Loop restaurant is the upscale anchor for dinner. Craftwood Inn (in a 1927 Arts and Crafts building) does destination dining in a remarkable setting. Spa at the Historic Cliff House Hotel is one of the better spas in Colorado. Garden of the Gods Trading Post is the local gallery-and-gift anchor for the tourist trade. The Emma Crawford Coffin Races (October) and other local festivals are genuinely beloved community events, not just tourist spectacles.

Reference Points from KC

Manitou Springs' Manitou Avenue is the closest thing in this guide to a purely independent Westport or 39th Street corridor — every single business is locally owned, and the community has fought to keep it that way. The residential character is closer to a quirky, hilly version of Hyde Park: mid-century homes, artistic expression visible in the landscaping and exterior design, genuine pride of ownership that's expressive rather than conformist. The scale is smaller than any KC neighborhood, which is both the charm and the limitation.

Honest Tradeoffs for Someone from PV/KC
  • The most independently-owned commercial district in this entire guide — literally zero chain businesses, beautiful mineral springs throughout the walkable core, and a genuine arts/hippie community that has resisted commodification
  • One hour from Denver, walkable within town, extraordinary natural setting (Pikes Peak, Garden of the Gods minutes away), and at a price point below most mountain towns
  • You're politically and culturally embedded in Colorado Springs metro — 5 minutes from a large, conservative city that shapes the broader social context; Manitou is a progressive enclave within a conservative region
  • Inventory is very tight — 5,500 people, limited developable land, and real demand means waiting for the right property and potentially paying above ask; this is not a casual buyer's market
  • No skiing and no easy path to regular skiing; if the mountain lifestyle you're after includes regular ski days, Manitou is not the base for that

Typical Homes & Architecture

Manitou's housing is constrained by its canyon geography — the town is threaded between the base of Pikes Peak and the creek, so development climbs the steep hillsides above the commercial district. Victorian and early-20th-century homes exist near the commercial core; craftsman bungalows occupy the gentler slopes; and contemporary construction perches on the hillsides with views but limited lot size. The architectural language is eclectic, expressive, and individual — homes here look like their owners had opinions, which they did. Garages are often absent or converted; mountain lots rarely accommodate the suburban garage culture of PV.
