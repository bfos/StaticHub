Fifteen options built around your four constraints — a genuinely relaxing resort with good food, a pool worth spending all day at, half-day hiking within reach, and not Cancún or Cabo — each priced all-in for three people, flights included, against your $3,000-per-person ceiling. Every property here has been checked for the one thing that quietly disqualifies a lot of the obvious candidates: whether your daughter is old enough to stay at all.

The short answer

  1. Riviera Nayarit is the answer to your brief — Punta de Mita and the Sayulita / San Pancho stretch. It is one stop from MCI into Puerto Vallarta, it is not Cabo or Cancún, the jungle hiking in the Sierra de Vallejo is genuinely good, and it is in Nayarit — a State Department Level 2 state, while Puerto Vallarta itself sits in Level 3 Jalisco.
  2. Marival Armony is the single best fit for how your daughter described what she wants: a true all-inclusive with one- and two-bedroom suites, five pools, all-ages, and a real teen program — she gets her own bedroom and can get her own food without anyone paying for anything. Roughly $2,270 per person for six nights, all in.
  3. Amomoxtli holds up better than a quick look suggests, with two caveats. The pool faces the Tepozteco cliffs, the food is regional and genuinely good, and its 13+ policy means your daughter qualifies. But it is a quiet 42-room retreat with no other teenagers, no all-inclusive, and its rooms sleep two — so "her own room" means a second room at full price. Details in option 10.

About her age — this matters more than it should

She turns 17 on March 17, which means she checks in at 16. Hotels apply their minimum age at check-in, not mid-stay, so 16 is the number that governs every booking below. That's a narrower door than 17 in one direction and a wider one in another:

13+ propertiesFine. This is what keeps Amomoxtli in play.
16+ adults-onlyFine — she qualifies on day one. Worth asking about explicitly.
18+ adults-onlyOut. This removes Paradero, Hotel San Cristóbal and Kimpton Mas Olas.
"Adults-only" unspecifiedAssume 18+ until they confirm in writing. Some listings use the label for 13+ or 16+ properties.

None of the fifteen options below turn on this — every one of them accepts a 16-year-old. But it changes what you should ask for on the phone, and it means one more thing worth knowing: at every property here she is priced as an adult. Nearly all of them charge full adult rates from age 13, so the "third person" line never gets a discount. That is precisely why a suite usually beats a second room.

What we ruled out, and why

Some of these are genuinely painful exclusions — one of them would otherwise have been the best property on the list for her.

Level 4 — do not travel

Ixtapa & Zihuatanejo

The painful one. Club Med Ixtapa Pacific is close to a perfect answer to your daughter's brief — all-inclusive, a teen club for ages 11–17 where she could come and go freely, trapeze, kayaking, several pools. Viceroy Zihuatanejo and the eco-resort Playa Viva are also excellent. All of them sit in Guerrero, which the State Department currently rates Level 4, Do Not Travel. Not a call we'd make for you, but you should make it knowingly.

Level 4 — do not travel

Manzanillo & the southern Costalegre

Colima is also Level 4. The Jalisco half of the Costalegre — Careyes, Las Alamandas — is technically available but is remote, very expensive, and a 2½–3 hour transfer from PVR for a coastline that isn't better than Nayarit's.

Fails the age check

Baja's three best pools

Paradero Todos Santos is, on paper, exactly your trip: a rate that bundles guided hiking and biking, a Michelin-recognized kitchen, and a spectacular desert pool. It is 18+ to check in. So is Hotel San Cristóbal Baja, and so is Kimpton Mas Olas — the reopened Rancho Pescadero, now 94 rooms with private plunge pools, and adults-only. Between them they take the three best pools in the region off the table, which is why the Baja options here rank where they do.

Weak on hiking

San Miguel de Allende

Live Aqua is a lovely hotel and the food scene is superb, but the "hiking" is really the Charco del Ingenio botanical garden and a canyon walk out at Cañada de la Virgen. Guanajuato is Level 3 as well. Great trip, wrong brief.

Excluded by preference

Cancún, Riviera Maya, Los Cabos

You ruled these out and we'd agree independently: none of them has real hiking. The nearest thing on the Caribbean side is cenote swimming and the Cobá ruins, which is a fine day but not a trail.

No resort to speak of

Copper Canyon, Huasteca Potosina, Chiapas

The three best hiking regions in Mexico, and none of them has a resort with a pool you'd want to spend a day beside. Huasteca Potosina in particular — turquoise rivers and waterfalls in a Level 2 state — is worth remembering for a future trip built around the hiking instead of the resort.

What we'd do next

  1. Set fare alerts on all four airports this week. Six months out is roughly the right booking window for spring break; the bands in the flights table will either firm up or move, and you'll want to know which before you commit to a region.
  2. Email Marival Armony and Conrad Punta de Mita directly for a March 14–20, 2027 quote for two adults and one 16-year-old, asking specifically for the one-bedroom suite rate and the two-connecting-rooms rate. Every property here charges her as an adult, so the suite is almost always the cheaper way to give her a door that closes. Say her age plainly in the first email — it's the fastest way to surface a policy problem before you're attached to a property.
  3. Confirm Amomoxtli's age policy in writing if it stays on the list. Sources describe it as 13+, but some booking channels list it as "adults only," which is how they flag 13+ properties — worth a direct email rather than a surprise at check-in.
  4. Check the Grand Velas blackout. Their published blackout runs March 20 – April 4, 2027, so a stay there has to end by the 19th. That is compatible with your window, but only if you book the front half of it.
  5. Passports. All three of you need one valid well past the travel dates, and hers must still be valid in her current name and photo — a minor's passport is issued for five years, so check the expiry now rather than in February. A minor traveling with both parents needs no notarized consent letter; that requirement only applies when a minor travels with one parent or neither.

How the costs were built. Re-check before booking; they change.

One genuine seasonal note. If Valle de Bravo appeals, the monarch butterflies at the Piedra Herrada sanctuary are a mid-November to mid-March phenomenon. March 13–21 is the very tail of that. You might catch the last of them in the first days of your window; by the 21st the sanctuary is likely closed or nearly empty. Don't build the trip around it.
