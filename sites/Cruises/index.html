A second try at sea

Six lines that fix what went wrong on the Discovery Princess — costed side by side for two adults and one sixteen-year-old, all in one cabin, on a warm Caribbean week.

7 nights Caribbean Party of 3 · one cabin Target: well under $11k all-in

The brief

What we're solving for

Last year's cruise was your most expensive trip and your lowest-rated. The ports were fine — the ship let you down. Every fix below traces back to one move up in tier and a warmer route.

What disappointed you

Mediocre food, slow service (15–30 min for a single app-ordered drink), a drink package you lost ~$150 each on, lame entertainment, a smoking casino, dinner-line waits, and pools too cool to enjoy.

foodservicevalueentertainmentsmokecool pools

What you actually want

Quiet luxury. Reading by the pool, music, and food and drink that arrives without a fight. No water-slides, characters, crooners, or cheesy lessons. Worth a bit more for an experience that beats a staycation.

The party — updated

Two adults plus one daughter aged 16; the 19-year-old sits this one out. All three share one cabin — which, as it happens, erases the booking headache from last time (see the note below).

Two settings worth changing

Sail the Caribbean, not the Pacific — your cool-pool problem was the Mexican-Riviera route. And step up one tier: nearly every complaint above is a mass-market-staffing symptom.

The shortlist, in detail

Two ways to step up — and the splurges

There are two genuinely different moves above Celebrity, both teen-friendly. A luxury enclave gives you a private bubble on a big, lively ship; an all-inclusive luxury line is smaller and quieter with near 1:1 service. Then there are the splurges worth knowing about.

MSC Yacht Club

Luxury enclave · best fit

Est. trip total $5,100–8,100 · all-inclusive

A keycard-only enclave of suites with a private restaurant, pool, sundeck, lounge and a 24/7 butler — while the full ship outside keeps the energy and teen spaces your 16-year-old wants. Booking all three of you into one Yacht Club suite (many sleep three) is where the value really lands: premium drinks, WiFi, butler and tips are all in the fare, and the butler model is the structural cure for the slow service that defined last year.

✓

✓

Teens: full run of the main ship and its teen venues; Yacht Club areas are members-only, but she's booked in with you.

Celebrity · Always Included

Premium · the value step-up

Est. trip total $4,300–6,150 · 3 in one balcony

The cleanest upgrade from Princess that stays well under budget. Food and design are a clear tier above what you had, drinks/WiFi/tips are bundled, and — directly answering your casino gripe — Celebrity runs fully smoke-free casinos across the fleet. Target the newer Edge-class ships (Apex, Beyond, Ascent).

Good news on booking

With all three in one cabin, the headache is gone

Last time, splitting the family across rooms was a hassle — and in the earlier draft of this report it was a real obstacle, because cruise lines require a guest under 21 to be in (or directly next to) a cabin with a 21+ adult. Putting all three of you in one suite sidesteps that rule completely.

Why it's simple now: your 16-year-old is in the same cabin as you, so every line's minor-supervision rule is satisfied automatically — no connecting-cabin requirements, no waivers, no enclave-access conflicts. You book one suite and you're done.

For a future trip with both daughters: the split you asked about (luxury for the parents, a standard room for the kids) only gets tricky while someone is under 18. Once both are 21+, you can book the parents into a Yacht Club or Haven suite and the daughters into any standard cabin you like, with no adjacency rules at all.

The recommendation

Where I'd point you

For the experience you described — quiet luxury, real service, food and drink that simply arrive — and a teen who still wants a fun ship around her, MSC Yacht Club is the standout, and pricing the three of you into one suite brings it in around $5,100–8,100 all-in: a true butler-served luxury experience for roughly what a comparable Princess-Plus trip costs, and comfortably under your $11k ceiling.

If you'd rather spend as little as possible while still clearly beating last year, Celebrity's Always Included on an Edge-class ship is the value play at $4,300–6,150 — better food, a smoke-free casino, drinks and tips bundled.

Consider Regent only if you decide to splurge and like that one number covers excursions, drinks, tips and a hotel night — its near 1:1 service is the real antidote to everything slow about last year, though it lands well over budget.

Notes & caveats

  • All figures are planning estimates for three guests, one cabin, seven nights in the Caribbean. Cruise pricing moves constantly with date, ship, cabin category and promotions — treat these as ballpark and confirm with a live quote before deciding.
  • Cruise fare covers the room with three guests, including each line's standard inclusive bundle where it has one. Drinks + WiFi and Upgrades & gratuities show what's already in the fare versus what's an unavoidable or optional extra.
  • Travel assumes round-trip air from Kansas City to a Florida or San Juan port (~$750–1,200 for three) plus one pre-cruise hotel night and transfers. Regent includes the hotel night in its fare, so only air is shown there.
  • Age rules reflect current published policies for sailings calling at a U.S. port, where "adult" means 21+; all are satisfied by the three of you sharing one cabin. Re-confirm at booking.
  • Weather: Eastern and especially Southern Caribbean routes (e.g. round-trip San Juan) run warmest and most pool-reliable — the opposite of your cool Mexican-Riviera experience.
