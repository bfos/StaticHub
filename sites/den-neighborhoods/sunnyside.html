Overview

Sunnyside sits just north of LoHi, bounded roughly by I-70 on the north, Federal Boulevard on the west, the South Platte River on the east, and 38th Avenue on the south. It is one of Denver's more densely populated inner neighborhoods — small lots, narrow streets, dense blocks — and has one of the highest walk scores of any neighborhood in this guide.

Sunnyside has historically been a working-class immigrant neighborhood, with a significant Mexican-American community that dates to the early 20th century. That history is still visible — in taquerias and Mexican bakeries alongside the newer coffee shops and cocktail bars. The neighborhood has gentrified significantly since 2010 (home prices up 9%+ year-over-year through 2025), and there is real tension between longtime residents and new arrivals, but it has not yet been hollowed out of its character the way some Denver neighborhoods have.

What Sunnyside offers that is rare in Denver is density plus walkability plus genuine character. This doesn't feel like a sanitized, curated neighborhood — it feels real. The streets are busy, there are dogs and children and people sitting on front porches, and the commercial mix on Tejon Street and 44th Avenue is eclectic rather than uniform.

Culture & Feel

Sunnyside's commercial activity is distributed across several nodes rather than one primary strip, which gives it a different feel than Platt Park or Berkeley. The Tejon Street corridor and 44th Avenue both have clusters of neighborhood businesses. Notable spots include Bacon Social House (beloved brunch institution), Our Mutual Friend Brewing (one of Denver's best craft breweries), The Lobster Bar, and multiple authentic Mexican restaurants that predate the gentrification wave.

The coffee culture is strong: Novo Coffee has a location here, and there are several neighborhood-specific cafes that serve as community anchors. The cocktail bar scene is developing — several well-reviewed spots have opened in the 2020s — but Sunnyside leans more neighborhood-bar than destination-cocktail-lounge.

Park access is a relative weakness compared to Wash Park or Sloan's Lake — there's no signature green space within the neighborhood. However, Rocky Mountain Lake Park (in adjacent Berkeley) is easily walkable, and the South Platte River trail along the eastern edge provides a substantial off-street biking and running corridor connecting downtown, LoHi, and points north.

The social character of the neighborhood is genuinely mixed: longtime Latino families, artists, young professionals, and a growing number of families with young children who were priced out of LoHi or Berkeley. Weekend mornings on the residential streets have the front-porch energy of an older urban neighborhood — the kind of thing that's nearly impossible to manufacture and that PV residents who have spent time in older cities instinctively recognize.

Kansas City Comparables

Sunnyside most closely resembles Westwood (KC, KS) or Roeland Park in the trajectory sense: a neighborhood with authentic working-class character that is actively gentrifying and attracting creative/professional residents drawn to the low prices and high walkability. It also has the energy of older West Side KC — a historically Latino neighborhood where new restaurants and residents have arrived while original community members remain. In pure physical terms, the bungalow streetscapes feel like Waldo's less-polished blocks, with the crucial difference that Sunnyside is more densely built and more genuinely walkable.

Honest Tradeoffs for the PV/Brookside Sensibility
  • Sunnyside is grittier than PV by a significant margin. The neighborhood has real urban density — small lots, tight streets, chain-link fences alongside picket fences, older cars parked nose-to-tail on narrow streets. If you want the PV quiet, Sunnyside will feel loud. This can read as energy or chaos depending on your disposition.
  • The gentrification dynamics create social complexity that PV residents are not accustomed to. Longtime residents and newcomers do not always mix easily, and there is community activism around displacement. This is not unique to Sunnyside but it's more visible here than in already-gentrified neighborhoods.
  • The neighborhood does not have a single dominant park the way Wash Park or Sloan's Lake does. Green space is distributed and modest.
  • Quality varies block to block more than in more established neighborhoods. A well-maintained block of bungalows can be directly adjacent to a block with deferred maintenance and industrial uses near the Platte. Do your street-by-street research carefully.
  • At $800K median, prices have risen quickly. The value proposition that made Sunnyside a bargain in 2015 is partly gone. You're still buying below Platt Park or Wash Park, but not by as much as you once could.

Typical Homes & Architecture

Sunnyside's housing is similar to Berkeley's in age and type: primarily 1910s–1940s craftsman bungalows and brick cottages, with some Denver Squares on larger lots. Lots are generally smaller (4,000–6,000 sq ft) than in Wash Park or Platt Park. There is more variation in condition than in more established neighborhoods — you'll find immaculately renovated bungalows next to properties that haven't been touched in decades.
