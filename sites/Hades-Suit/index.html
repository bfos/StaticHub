01

What Hades is actually wearing

Worth pinning down before shopping, because two details usually get remembered wrong — and both change what you buy.

Black, not grey

Designer Michael Krass used black cloth with silver threading in the stripes — the shine is doing half the work. A matte black-and-white pinstripe reads as a banker; the lustre is what reads as underworld.

Huey Long, not Capone

Krass has said the reference was a photo of the Depression-era Louisiana politician in pinstripes. Power dressing, not gangster cosplay — a useful line to stay on at a wedding.

Sleeve garters

Your "arm belts." Patrick Page suggested them, to say working-class man who made it. They sit at mid-bicep over the shirt sleeve, and there are two of them.

Silver shirt, red flower

The shirt under the vest is silver, not white. In Act II a red carnation goes in the vest pocket — a one-item detail that will do more for the reference than anything else on this page.

02

The pinstripes

The vest and trousers are the whole outfit; everything else is trim. Two honest routes: made-to-measure real wool, or cheap off-the-rack plus a tailor. Buying a three-piece and leaving the jacket at home is a legitimate move — it's often the only way to get a vest in the same cloth.

    Exactly the right look — jet black, chalk-width white stripe, vest included — at a price that leaves real room for tailoring. Budget $60–100 to have the vest and trousers taken in — an off-the-rack vest that fits badly will sink the outfit faster than the cloth will.

03

The shirt

This is where most attempts at this look fall apart, and it's a construction problem, not a taste problem. A soft point or spread collar collapses when you unbutton it — it has nothing holding it up, so it lies flat against your chest and reads as "forgot to finish getting dressed." You need either a camp collar, which is cut to sit open, or a stiffer collar in a fabric with body. Satin drapes beautifully and flops worst of all.

    $138

    The literal reference Made to measure Thomas Mason, Italy

    Thomas Mason is a genuinely serious shirting mill, and this is the silver shirt the costume actually uses. Made to measure means you can specify a cutaway or spread collar and a fuller sleeve in the same order as the vest and trousers — one shipment, one set of measurements. Their black is the same price if you'd rather the shirt disappear under the vest.

The arm belts

Real sleeve garters, worn as a pair at mid-bicep. Mechanically they exist to pull excess sleeve up and blouse it above the elbow — which is exactly the silhouette you're after, and which means they only work over a shirt with sleeve fabric to spare. On a slim-fit shirt there's nothing to gather and they just look like straps.

06

Fit notes that decide this

Ask for a full back on the vest

Suit waistcoats usually have a satin back and a cinch buckle, because a jacket hides it. You have no jacket. People will see your back all night — specify the striped cloth on the back too, or accept that half your outfit is lining.

The vest must cover the waistband

Jacketless, the vest hem is the outfit's visible waistline. A gap of shirt between vest and trousers is the single most common failure. Err long, and have the tailor check it seated.

Regular-fit sleeves, not slim

Garters need slack to gather. If you order the shirt made-to-measure, ask for a standard or generous sleeve even if you take everything else trim.

Higher rise, gentle pleat

Depression-era tailoring sat at the natural waist. A slightly higher rise reads period; a low flat-front reads office. It also means the vest meets the trousers without a fight.

Two buttons open, not four

Hades is open at the collar, not to the sternum. Undo the top two, leave the rest — the vest frames the opening and does the work. This is the difference between bold and costume.

Boots, and skip the sunglasses

The costume's white snakeskin cowboy boots are the fun part — a black or grey snake-embossed boot gets you there without a white shoe at a wedding. Sunglasses indoors is where "guest in a great outfit" becomes "guy doing a bit."

07

Order sequence

The long pole isn't shipping — it's the measurement confirmation email that made-to-measure shops send after you order. It stops the clock until you reply.

  1. This week

    Order the leather garters first. Made to order, shipping internationally, and the cheapest thing to be early on.

  2. This week

    Place the pinstripe order. Measure a vest and a pair of trousers that already fit you and submit those numbers rather than body measurements — every StudioSuits reviewer who had a problem had measured their body.

  3. Days 2–5

    Answer the confirmation email same-day. They query anything that looks off. Their 2–3 week clock is explicitly conditional on confirmed measurements.

  4. Week 2–3

    Everything lands. Try the full stack on together — vest, shirt, garters — not piece by piece.

  5. Week 3–4

    Tailor window. Only needed on Build B, or if a made-to-measure piece missed. Keep a week of slack here.

  6. Day before

    Steam, don't press. A hot iron flattens the lustre out of a striped worsted and you lose the shine that makes the whole thing work. Buy the carnation the morning of.
